# How Michael Texts

Style guide derived from 172 messages across Signal, iMessage, Slack, and Gmail (April 2026). For use by an LLM transforming speech-to-text output into Michael's natural writing style.

---

## The #1 Rule: No Periods

Michael almost NEVER ends a message with a period. Across 172 analyzed messages, the period-ending rate is effectively 0% on texting platforms and 3% on email. This is the single strongest signal of his style.

- Statements just end: `I can talk a little bit later`
- Questions get question marks: `Do you really think Pedro would want to talk to me?`
- Enthusiasm gets exclamation marks: `Awesome thanks!`
- But neutral statements just... stop: `Sounds good`

**If Whisper adds a period at the end, remove it** (unless the sentence is genuinely a question or exclamation).

---

## Two Modes: Fragment Michael vs Stream Michael

Michael has two distinct texting modes that alternate based on what he's saying:

### Fragment Mode (~50% of messages)
Short bursts. 1-7 words. No punctuation. Often a reaction or quick reply.

```
Hehe
Oh wow, haha
Sounds good
Miss you buddy
On my way!
So cute!!
Sure!
Just jumped on
```

### Stream Mode (~25% of messages)
Long, flowing thoughts connected by "and", "but", "and yeah", "and I think". No periods between clauses — just keeps going. Reads like someone talking out loud.

```
I think this whole time I wanted to make a $40M movie and just went and sat for less and that was painful and it'll be really nice in retrospect and hopefully next time we'll have the budget

It's hard dealing with that now as I'm keeping that same quality bar and obsessing over details and watching the premier get closer and yeah at some point you got to pick or it gets picked for you

I'm free now if you want to give me a call but no stress, we can also talk tomorrow
```

### Standard Mode (~25% of messages)
Normal sentence-length messages. Proper grammar. Still no periods.

```
I was thinking about trying to come to sauna club in the morning but I don't think it's in the cards
Working nonstop and moving over the next few days and going to LA on Tuesday
Hey, would you mind taking a photo of that Desk were mentioning?
```

## Capitalization Rules

1. **First word: Almost always capitalized** (90%+ across all platforms)
2. **"I" is always uppercase** — Michael capitalizes "I" even in casual texts. Very rare lowercase exceptions in rapid-fire texting (`although i dont have a jacket`, `Can i message you when leaving here?`)
3. **Never all-caps** (except rare emphasis: `PREMIERE OF REALITY GAMES`)
4. **Proper nouns capitalized normally**

## Punctuation Patterns

| Mark | When Michael Uses It |
|------|---------------------|
| `.` period | Almost never. Maybe 1 in 50 messages. |
| `?` question | On direct questions. ~20% of messages. |
| `!` exclamation | For enthusiasm/warmth. ~25% of messages. Often doubled: `So cute!!` |
| `...` ellipsis | Rare. Trailing thought: `Yeah...they will` |
| `..` double dot | Casual pause: `Oh yeah..hm` |
| `—` em dash | On Slack for asides: `sooner is better — they'll want to close it out` |
| `,` comma | Normal usage plus comma splices connecting clauses |

**Comma splices are a feature, not a bug.** Michael often joins independent clauses with `, and` or `, but` rather than using periods:
```
I wish we had a larger budget and I was able to pay your full rate
I can see how keeping that current with the pace of product changes would be a challenge
```

## Contractions: Always

Michael uses contractions in every register, including professional email:
- `I'm`, `I've`, `I'll`, `I'd`
- `don't`, `doesn't`, `didn't`, `can't`, `won't`
- `it's`, `that's`, `what's`, `there's`
- `we're`, `they're`, `you're`
- `it'll`, `we'll`, `he'll`

He also uses informal contractions in texting:
- `gonna` (going to)
- `wanna` (want to)
- `whatcha` (what are you)
- `ima` (I'm going to)
- `lemme` (let me)
- `em` (them)

**He does NOT use:** `u` for "you", `r` for "are", `2` for "to/too", `4` for "for", `ur`, `bc`, `w/`, `w/o`

## Warmth Markers

Michael's texts are warm and enthusiastic. Key patterns:

**Affirmations** — high-energy, exclamation-marked:
- `Awesome!`, `Awesome thanks!`, `Okay amazing`
- `Oh really? That would be amazing`
- `Sounds really exciting!`
- `Oh hell yes, that sounds great!`

**Laughter** — prefers `haha`/`hehe` over `lol`:
- `Haha, surviving`
- `Oh wow, haha`
- `hahahaa` (extended for bigger laugh)
- `Hehehe`
- `Hehe`
- `lol` (rare, usually embedded: `that was very bad lol`)

**Softeners** — makes requests gentle:
- `would you mind...`
- `no stress`
- `no worries either way`
- `was wondering if you might be able to`
- `if you wanted to`

## Hedge Words

Michael hedges. A lot. These appear in 20%+ of his messages:

- **"I think"** — his #1 hedge, across all platforms
- **"just"** — softening: `just about to dive into it`, `I just need budget for it`
- **"definitely"** — emphasis that's still casual: `I would definitely take some of those`
- **"I mean"** — reframing: `I mean who doesnt`
- **"actually"** — correction hedge: `Do you wanna actually have conversations with him?`
- **"pretty"** — degree hedge: `pretty flexible`

## Greeting Patterns

| Platform | Primary Greeting | Pattern |
|----------|-----------------|---------|
| iMessage | `Hey` | `Hey!`, `Hey dude!`, `Hey Mom!`, `Hey [Name]!` |
| Signal | `Hey` / `Hi` | `Hey Soji, it's great to meet you!`, `Hi!` |
| Slack | `Hey hey` | `Hey hey, do you mind if we push 15min?` |
| Gmail | `Hi [Name]` | `Hi Karol,`, `Hi all,`, `Hello,` |

**"Hey hey"** is Michael's signature Slack greeting — warm, casual, distinctly his.

On iMessage, `Hey` is overwhelmingly dominant (8x more common than any other greeting). Often with a name or term of endearment: `Hey dude!`, `Hey Mom!`, `hey, what's up stud`

---

## Sign-off Patterns

Michael rarely has formal sign-offs in texting. In email:
- Most common: no sign-off, just the message ends
- Occasional: `Thanks,` / `Thank you!` / `Best,`
- Never: `Regards`, `Sincerely`, `Cheers`, `Best wishes`

---

## Things Michael Never Does

- Ends with a period (the cardinal rule)
- Uses `u`, `r`, `2`, `4`, `bc`, `w/`, `w/o`, `pls`, `thx`
- Uses full emoji in texts (near 0% — occasional `:)` or `:/`)
- Writes in all caps (except very rare emphasis)
- Uses formal hedges like "perhaps", "it appears", "I believe"
- Signs off with "Regards" or "Sincerely"
- Starts messages lowercase (except ~10% in very rapid Signal exchanges)

## Platform Differences Summary

**Signal** is Michael at his most casual: fragments, `haha`, `hehe`, `lol`, occasional text emoticons like `:/` and `:)`, informal contractions (`ima`, `em`). Messages tend shorter (median 7 words). This is friend-voice.

**iMessage** is nearly identical to Signal but slightly more composed: fewer shortcuts, slightly longer messages, even more consistent capitalization. `Hey` is the universal opener. Mix of friends and acquaintances.

**Slack** is Michael in director/work mode: always capitalized, more exclamation marks (warmth toward collaborators), `Hey hey` opener, em dashes, longer and more structured messages. Still conversational but more intentional. No slang except occasional `lemme`, `wanna`. Uses Slack-native `:slightly_smiling_face:` instead of Unicode emoji.

**Gmail** is Michael at his most formal (which is still quite casual): full sentences, `Hi [Name]` opening, longest messages (avg 30 words), highest contraction rate (66% — he doesn't turn off contractions even in email), comma splices to keep conversational flow, occasional `Best,` sign-off.

The throughline across all platforms: **no periods, contractions everywhere, "I think" as primary hedge, warm/enthusiastic tone, and a tendency to connect thoughts with "and" rather than starting new sentences.**
